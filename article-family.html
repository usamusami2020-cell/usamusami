Family life

At home, our truest behaviour

By Usman bin Affan Khan · usamusami.com

Outside the home, most of us are careful. We smile, we choose our words, we keep our temper. At home the guard comes down, and the people who love us most often meet the tired, irritable version of us. Yet it is behaviour at home that shows who we really are.

The Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) taught that the best of people are those who are best to their families, and he was, by his own words, the best to his own family. Kindness, in Islam, is not a public performance. It begins at the dinner table, in the doorway, in the ordinary hour.

A few habits carry a long way. Greet your family warmly when you enter, and let the first words of the evening be soft ones. Listen fully before answering, especially when someone is upset: being heard is often half the healing. When you are tired, say so honestly instead of letting the tiredness speak in a harsh tone. Apologise quickly when you are wrong; a short, sincere apology protects a home more than a long argument won. Thank your family for small things, the meal, the tea, the errand, because gratitude noticed aloud becomes love felt.

Give time, not only money. Ten unhurried minutes with a parent, a spouse, a brother or a sister, with the phone put away, tells them they matter. Most family wounds do not come from one big event. They come from small coldness repeated daily, and they heal the same way: small warmth, repeated daily.

A peaceful home is not found. It is built, by everyone in it, one gentle day at a time.
