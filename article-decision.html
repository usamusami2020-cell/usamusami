Mind and daily life

A place to say what is on your mind: deciding amid doubt and strain

By Usman bin Affan Khan · usamusami.com

There are times in life when the mind gets stuck on a single decision. Shall I do this, or shall I do that? Days pass in this doubt, and the mind slowly grows heavy.

Doubt does not mean you are weak. Doubt means the mind is thinking carefully; it does not want to make a mistake. That care is a good thing, but staying stuck in it for a lifetime is not.

Often the real cause of the doubt is not the decision itself, but the strain within relationships. The fear that someone will be hurt, that someone will be angry, is what holds the mind back.

The first task is to make what is on your mind clear to yourself. Write three things down on paper: what I want, what I fear, and what I do not want to lose. Once it is written, much of the tangle in the mind begins to loosen.

Then speak with a person you trust. Saying what is on your mind out loud lightens its weight. If there is no one to tell, write it in a diary; set out the thoughts of your own mind in an orderly way.

Before deciding, ask two questions. Does this decision agree with my faith, and will it keep the peace of my family? When both answers come together, the mind settles a great deal.

A big decision does not have to be taken all at once. Take one small step, see its result, and then take the next step. The success of a small step gives the mind its confidence back.

Consultation with Allah is the greatest companion of all. Pray istikhara, make dua, and then move along the path where the mind feels at peace. Tawakkul does not mean giving up effort; it means making the effort and leaving the result in His hands.

There is no gain in blaming yourself. If a decision turns out wrong, it can be put right; but if you sit in doubt for a lifetime, the time itself is lost.

And if the mind stays stuck in doubt and strain for many days, and sleep and daily work begin to suffer, speak with a qualified specialist. Asking for help is not weakness; it is taking care of yourself.

Take one small decision today: open your heart and say one thing that is on your mind to a person you trust, and take one small decision of your own and begin its first step. That small step is the beginning of breaking the doubt.

This writing is educational only. It is not a diagnosis or a treatment, and it never replaces a qualified professional.
