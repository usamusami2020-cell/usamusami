Speaking with children so they listen and grow

By Usman bin Affan Khan · usamusami.com

Every parent and elder wants the same thing: that children listen, learn good manners, and grow into confident, kind people. The surprise is that children learn behaviour less from our instructions and more from watching us. The way we speak to them slowly becomes the way they speak to themselves.

Start with connection before correction. Come down to the child's level, look at them, and speak in a calm voice. A child who feels shouted at hears the shouting, not the lesson. Short, clear words work better than long lectures: say what to do, not only what not to do. “Walk slowly” teaches more than “Do not run” repeated ten times.

Listen to them fully, even when the story is small. When children feel heard at home, they keep talking to us as they grow, and that open door is worth more than any single rule. Name their feelings for them: “You are upset because your toy broke.” A child who can name a feeling is on the way to managing it.

Praise effort and good behaviour specifically and warmly: “You shared your food, that was kind.” Praise tells the child exactly what to repeat. When correction is needed, correct the act, not the child, and never shame them in front of others. Keep your promises to them, small as they are, because trust is built in small coins.

Above all, be patient. Manners and habits grow the way plants grow, with water, light and time: your example, your kind words, and your steady presence. The child in front of you is learning how to be a person by watching the person you are.
